Streaming the live view of a tab in a person's own Chrome no longer changes how the tab looks. Each frame used to be a clipped screenshot scaled to the viewer's size, and Chrome takes a clipped screenshot by redrawing the tab itself at that size until it answers. On a 2x display, the tab the person was looking at shrank into its top-left corner on every frame (a blink several times a second). A tab Chrome was not drawing (behind another tab) never answered its capture, so the next frame's capture began on top of it, took the shrunken size for the tab's own and put it back last: the tab stayed drawn small until its window was resized. Frames of attached Chrome are now whole-viewport captures, which leave the tab untouched, scaled down to the viewer's size in browserd. For every browser, a capture of a tab is now sent only after the previous one is answered or its connection gives up on it (full-page screenshots included), within its own deadline, so a capture no longer begins on top of a slow one and leaves the tab at that capture's size. A live-view frame that finds a screenshot of the tab still running skips that round instead of counting as a failed frame, so a slow screenshot does not end the live view.
