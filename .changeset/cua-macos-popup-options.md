On macOS, choosing an option of a dropdown no longer opens or leaves open its menu in a window behind the person's. Invoking an option of a dropdown (an item of its open menu, or the option a closed browser dropdown shows) chooses that option on the dropdown, as `set_value` does, instead of pressing the item: a browser dropdown took such a press as a choice and opened its menu again, raising its window, and a closed one's option could bring the browser down. `set_value` on a browser dropdown in a background window of the front browser now types the option's text instead of opening the menu, a menu still open after a choice is closed, and a pointer click on the option a closed browser dropdown shows is refused like one on the dropdown. A dropdown in a window that is already in front opens its menu instead of being refused, and an ARIA menu button (whose menu is drawn in the page) is no longer refused as a browser dropdown; the refusal that remains names `set_value`, and the window's own `focus` action with its target to open the menu itself, only when that focus would bring the window forward (not while someone else is using the computer). An option with no text to choose it by is refused in the background instead of pressed. A native combo box reads as a `combobox` that takes `set_value`, and its app takes the new value as chosen.
