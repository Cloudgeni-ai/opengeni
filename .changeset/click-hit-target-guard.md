A browser click no longer lands on a different element while reporting success on the one it was asked to click. Hit-testing happens before the pointer moves, and a page can put something over the target in between: an open popover that follows its anchor back after the driver scrolled the panel to uncover the target, or a hover card that the pointer's arrival opens. The driver now waits for scroll-driven layout to settle before trusting a reveal scroll. Each click also runs behind a guard in an isolated world: a press that would reach anything other than the target is cancelled together with its release and clicks, and the action fails with the existing "browser element is covered by …" error, which becomes an unknown outcome once pointer input has been sent. Releases and clicks are not judged by their own target, so pointer capture and label-forwarded clicks keep working. A guard that a JavaScript dialog interrupted is removed by the next action once the dialog is handled, and it disarms itself after five seconds regardless. The guard also reports each press's verdict out of the page as it happens, through a binding only its isolated world can call, so a verdict survives the document being replaced or removed before it is read back; a click whose verdict is missing is reported as an unknown outcome rather than a success.
