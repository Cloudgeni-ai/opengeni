A browser click, double-click, hover or drag that first scrolls its element into view now waits for the page to finish reacting to that scroll before it aims. Pages often change layout once they scroll (a sticky header that shrinks, directly, on the next frame or after a short debounce), which moved the element after it was measured, so the press landed on whatever took its place and the click was refused as covered at its center although nothing covered it. The driver now waits until the element's box has not changed for 150 ms of drawn frames, counted from the first frame after the scroll, or for at most 600 ms while it keeps moving; a busy browser whose frames come slowly is given until it has drawn two frames, at most 1.5 s by the driver's own clock. The wait runs in an isolated world, so page scripts that replace their timers or frame callbacks cannot hold it, including during a drag with the button held. An element already in view, which needs no scroll, and a hidden tab do not wait. When the press still falls outside the element's own box (for example the pointer's arrival collapses the panel above it), the refusal now says the element moved, names what the press fell on, says the click was not delivered and that it can be clicked again; a press inside the element's box that something else takes is still refused as covered at its center.
