# Reply patterns

Short patterns for replies that are not routine. The examples show shape, not
wording to copy; the user's own voice always wins.

## Length by channel

| Channel | Usual length |
|---|---|
| Chat or instant message | 1-4 sentences |
| Reply to a short email | about as long as the message it answers |
| Support ticket | 1-3 short paragraphs |
| Longer email | at most 3-5 short paragraphs |
| Senior reader | 2-3 short paragraphs, the decision first |

Over the limit, cut before you tighten: openers, restated background and
closing summaries go first.

## Tone by situation

| Situation | Aim for |
|---|---|
| Routine question | Direct answer, friendly close |
| Good news | Warm, specific, brief |
| Delay or bad news | The news in the first sentence, the reason, what happens next |
| Problem or outage | What you know, what you're doing, when they'll hear next |
| Billing or account | Factual and calm; no figures the user has not confirmed |
| Escalated or angry | Acknowledge first, own it, a concrete next contact |

## Patterns

**Routine question.** Answer, then close. No preamble.

> Hi Sara,
> Yes, the workshop is on the second floor, first door on the left.
> See you Thursday,
> Nils

**Promise coming due.** Name the promise; do not act as if the thread is new.

> Hi Tom,
> As promised, here are the updated figures. [attach]
> Nils

**Unhappy customer.** Acknowledge and commit to a next contact. No offer, no
blame, no fix until the user decides.

> Hi Marit,
> Sorry, that should not still be happening after our visit. I'm looking into
> it now and will call you by [time].
> Nils

Then tell the user this one needs them.

**Can't answer yet.** Honest and short, with when they'll hear back, using a
time the user can meet or a placeholder.

> Hi Ellen,
> I need to check the specifications before I answer properly. I'll get back
> to you by [day].
> Nils

**Declining.** Short, honest, and a referral when the user has one.

> Hi Pete,
> Thanks for thinking of us, but that's not work we take on. [Name] at
> [company] does it well.
> Nils

**Bad news or delay.** Lead with the news, give the reason in one sentence,
then what happens next.

**Feature or request you won't grant.** Acknowledge why they asked, say no
clearly, offer an alternative if one exists, and leave the door open only if
the user wants to.

**Following up after silence.** Give a specific reason to reply ("the quote
expires on [date]"), not "just following up". Keep it under 60 words.

**Scheduling.** Offer two or three concrete slots from the user's calendar or
instructions, or ask for theirs. Never invent a meeting link.

## Checks before handing over

- The first sentence answers the question.
- Every number, date and name is from the thread or the user.
- Nothing commits the user to money, a date or a remedy they have not chosen.
- Recipients are the ones the user expects.
- The draft sounds like the user's earlier messages, not like a template.
