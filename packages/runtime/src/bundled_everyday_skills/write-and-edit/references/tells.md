# Machine-writing tells

Read this during the finish pass of a substantial edit. These patterns make
text read as generated. Each one is fine once in a while from a careful
writer; act when a pattern repeats or several appear together. The same
patterns appear in every language, so treat their equivalents the same way.

Background reading: Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" page,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing

## Four fast checks

1. **Say it aloud.** Would the sender say this sentence to the reader's face?
   If it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it.
2. **Swap the name.** Could the text go to a different person or company with
   two words changed? Then it is a template. Add the specific reason for
   writing, or keep it short and honest.
3. **Foreign phrase.** Is there a phrase the writer never uses? One is enough
   to break the voice.
4. **Earned sentences.** Does every sentence tell the reader something new?
   Delete the ones that restate.

## Structure

- **Invented contrast.** "It's not just a tool, it's a partner." The first
  half denies something nobody said. State the real point once.
- **Echo endings.** A closing line or paragraph that repeats what was just
  said, or a row of short dramatic fragments. Cut it, or turn it into a real
  claim.
- **Announcements.** "Here's the thing", "Let's dive in", "It's worth noting
  that". Start with the point instead.
- **Arguing with no one.** Rebutting an objection the reader never raised.
- **Threes by habit.** Lists of three adjectives or three parallel examples
  when the meaning has one or two. Keep three only when there really are three.
- **Uniform shape.** Every paragraph the same length, or several sentences in a
  row starting the same way. Vary them.
- **Dashes everywhere.** Long dashes used as the default link between clauses.
  Use a period, comma, colon or parentheses. If the user's own sample uses
  dashes, match its rate.

## Words

- **Inflated importance:** pivotal, crucial, vital, testament, landscape,
  tapestry, realm, journey, game-changer.
- **Stock verbs:** delve, leverage, unlock, empower, elevate, foster,
  showcase, underscore, streamline, navigate (used figuratively).
- **Empty adjectives:** robust, seamless, cutting-edge, innovative, vibrant,
  world-class, meticulous.
- **Stacked hedges:** "could potentially", "may possibly", "arguably might".
  Keep one hedge where the doubt is real.
- **Vague credit:** "experts say", "studies show", "many believe" with no
  source. Name the source or drop the claim.

## Email and message habits

- Openers: "I hope this email finds you well", "I wanted to reach out", "I came
  across your company", "Quick question", a compliment about the reader's
  business. Open with the reason for writing.
- Filler: "just circling back", "touching base", "per my last email", "at your
  earliest convenience", "I'd love to", "excited to".
- Closers: "Please don't hesitate to reach out", "Let me know if you have any
  questions" in place of a real, specific ask. End with one small, concrete
  ask or none.

## Chat leftovers

Remove anything written to the person who asked for the text rather than to
its reader: "Certainly!", "Great question", "Here is your revised text",
"I hope this helps", offers of further versions, and notes about the previous
draft.

## Formatting

- Bold used for decoration, or a bold label in front of every bullet.
- Headings on a text short enough to read at a glance.
- Title Case On Every Heading when the destination uses sentence case.
- Emoji the writer would not use.

## Final fact check

After removing tells, confirm the rewrite did not add or drop any fact, name,
number, date, quote, source or promise. Removing a sentence that carried a
fact is an error; move the fact instead.
