# NDA fast path

Use this for a non-disclosure or confidentiality agreement. If the document
also sets prices, deliverables, exclusivity or other business terms, it is not
just an NDA: say so and review it as a full contract.

## Answer shape

- **Verdict:** Sign, Sign after changes, or Don't sign yet.
- **One line:** parties, mutual or one-way (and who is bound), how long it
  lasts, governing law.
- **Issues:** each with the exact clause quoted, the risk in plain words, and
  the fix to ask for.
- **Basis:** the workspace's own NDA positions if it has them, otherwise the
  market defaults below. Say which.
- The not-legal-advice line, naming anything worth a lawyer's look.

A clean NDA gets a short answer: "Sign. Standard mutual NDA, 2 years, nothing
unusual."

## Market defaults

A standard NDA usually has all of these:

- **Direction fits the deal.** Mutual when both sides share information;
  one-way only when just one side discloses, and then the user should be the
  protected party if they are the one disclosing.
- **Reasonable definition.** Non-public information shared for the stated
  purpose, marked or obviously confidential. Not "all information of any kind".
- **Standard exceptions.** Information that is or becomes public through no
  fault of the recipient; that the recipient already had; that the recipient
  develops independently; that the recipient receives lawfully from someone
  else; and disclosure required by law, with notice where allowed.
- **Sensible use and sharing.** Use only for the stated purpose. Sharing
  allowed with employees, advisers and contractors who need to know and are
  bound by similar duties.
- **Limited duration.** An agreement term of about 1-3 years and
  confidentiality lasting about 2-5 years after disclosure; longer only for
  genuine trade secrets.
- **Return or destroy** on request or at the end, with an exception for copies
  kept for legal or backup reasons.
- **Ordinary remedies.** Injunctive relief is normal. Fixed penalties or
  liquidated damages are not.
- **Reasonable governing law** in a familiar jurisdiction, with courts rather
  than mandatory arbitration for an ordinary NDA.

## Verdict rules

**Sign** when every default above holds, or the deviations are trivial.

**Sign after changes** when the NDA is basically sound but has fixable issues:
a broad definition, a longer but still common term (for example 5 years), one
missing exception that is easy to add, a narrowly limited residuals clause,
workable marking requirements, a missing retention exception, or a
non-preferred but acceptable governing law.

**Don't sign yet** when any of these appear:

- one-way in the wrong direction, when the user also discloses;
- no independent-development or legal-compulsion exception;
- a non-compete, non-solicit, exclusivity or standstill clause (outside an
  acquisition context);
- confidentiality without end, or 10 years or more, for ordinary information;
- a broad residuals clause that lets the other side use what it remembers;
- any licence or assignment of intellectual property;
- fixed penalties or liquidated damages per breach;
- audit rights over the user's systems or records;
- mandatory arbitration or courts somewhere unreasonable.

## Standard fixes

| Issue | Ask for |
|---|---|
| Overbroad definition | Limit to information marked confidential, or that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential |
| Missing independent development | Add the standard exception for information developed without use of the other side's information |
| Non-solicit of employees | Delete it; if they insist, limit to active targeted solicitation for 12 months, excluding general job adverts |
| Residuals clause | Delete it, or limit it to general know-how retained in unaided memory, excluding trade secrets, and granting no licence |
| No end date | A fixed period (for example 3 years from disclosure), with longer protection only for trade secrets |
| Fixed penalty per breach | Delete; rely on ordinary damages and injunctive relief |
| One-way against the user | Make it mutual |
