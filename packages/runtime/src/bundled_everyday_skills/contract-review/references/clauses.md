# Clause guide

What normal looks like, what to flag, and what to ask for. "Normal" means
common commercial practice, not any one country's law; when local law decides
the point, say so.

## Payment and cash flow

- **Normal:** payment within 30 days of invoice; clear invoicing rules; a
  late-payment interest clause; a price-adjustment mechanism for multi-year
  deals.
- **Flag:** 60 days or more (yellow), 90 days or more (red for a small
  business); payment only after acceptance "to the client's satisfaction";
  rigid invoice formats that let payment slip on technicalities; no late
  interest.
- **Ask for:** a fixed number of days from invoice; deemed acceptance after a
  set number of days without written objection.

## Liability and indemnities

- **Normal:** each side's total liability capped, often at the fees paid in
  the last 12 months; indirect and consequential losses excluded for both
  sides; narrow, mutual indemnities for third-party claims (for example
  intellectual-property infringement).
- **Flag:** no cap, a cap far below the value at risk (for example 1-3 months
  of fees), exceptions that swallow the cap, one-sided exclusions, indemnities
  for "any and all claims arising out of or relating to" the services.
- **Ask for:** "Each party's total liability under this agreement shall not
  exceed the fees paid or payable in the 12 months before the claim." Limit
  indemnities to third-party claims caused by the indemnifying party's breach
  or negligence.

## Term, renewal and exit

- **Normal:** termination for convenience on about 30 days' notice, or a
  short fixed term; termination for cause after a cure period of about 30
  days; payment for work done up to termination; a reasonable renewal notice
  window.
- **Flag:** long terms with no way out; automatic renewal with a long notice
  window (60-90 days before renewal) or renewal at a higher list price; no cure
  period; no payment for work in progress; obligations that survive
  indefinitely.
- **Ask for:** a renewal reminder or a shorter notice window, a renewal price
  cap (for example the previous price plus a stated percentage), mutual
  termination for convenience.

## Intellectual property

- **Normal:** each party keeps what it owned before; the customer owns or
  gets a broad licence to the paid-for deliverables; the supplier keeps its
  general tools, templates and know-how.
- **Flag:** assignment of pre-existing IP or background tools; "work product"
  defined to include drafts, notes and internal tools; broad feedback clauses
  giving the other side perpetual rights.
- **Ask for:** a background-IP carve-out, and a licence rather than assignment
  where the deliverable depends on the user's own tools.

## Data and confidentiality

- **Normal:** confidentiality for 2-5 years with standard exceptions; data
  processing terms when personal data is handled; the customer's data used
  only to provide the service; deletion or return at the end.
- **Flag:** "all information shared" with no exceptions; perpetual
  confidentiality; the supplier may use customer data to improve its products
  or train models; subprocessors not disclosed; no data processing terms when
  personal data is involved; data kept after the end without limit.
- **Ask for:** a use limitation ("solely to provide the services"), a data
  processing agreement, a subprocessor list with notice of changes, deletion
  within a set period after termination with export before.

## Scope and changes

- **Normal:** a clear scope, a written change process with price and time
  adjustments, objective acceptance criteria.
- **Flag:** vague scope, no change process (scope creep without pay),
  subjective acceptance, delays penalized for one side only.

## Restrictions

- **Normal:** no restrictions beyond confidentiality; at most a narrow
  non-solicit of each other's staff for a short period.
- **Flag:** non-competes, exclusivity, industry-wide non-solicits, most
  favoured customer pricing across the user's whole client base.

## Operational terms

- **Normal:** governing law and courts where one party is based; assignment
  only with consent, except to a successor; audits limited in scope and
  frequency with notice.
- **Flag:** a distant or unusual jurisdiction, mandatory arbitration with
  costly rules, unrestricted assignment by the other side, unlimited audits,
  insurance limits the user cannot meet.

## Plain words

| Contract says | Tell the user |
|---|---|
| Liability capped at 12 months' fees | If they cause a problem, the most we can recover is a year of what we paid them. |
| No termination for convenience | Once signed, we are locked in for the full term, even if we stop using it. |
| Auto-renewal with 60 days' notice | It renews automatically each year; to cancel we must tell them two months before the renewal date. |
| No IP indemnity | If someone claims their product infringes, the vendor is not obliged to defend us. |
| Subprocessors not disclosed | We do not know which other companies can access our data through them. |
| Data deleted 30 days after termination | Export anything we need before cancelling; after a month it is gone. |
| Service credits capped at 10% | If the service goes down we get a small credit, which will not cover the cost to us. |
| Indemnify "any and all claims" | We pay for their legal problems connected to our use of the service, even ones we did not cause. |

Avoid section numbers, defined terms in quotes and legal jargon in the plain
explanation; keep the exact wording only in the quote itself.
